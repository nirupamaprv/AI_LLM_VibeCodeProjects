# Medium Title Generator

Generates 10 scroll-stopping title + subtitle combinations for Medium articles published in **Niru's Notes** (account: nirupamaprv).

## Critical: Ask-First Workflow

**If the user says "title ideas" (or any trigger phrase) WITHOUT providing content or an outline, always ask for the topic outline FIRST before generating anything.**

Use this exact prompt:
> "What's the article about? Share a quick outline, a few bullet points, or even just the core idea — I'll use that to generate 10 title options for Niru's Notes."

Only proceed to generate titles once the user has provided their outline or topic.

## When to use this skill

- User says "title ideas" with or without accompanying content
- User shares a blog post draft, outline, idea, or raw notes and wants title options
- User explicitly asks for "Medium titles," "headline ideas," or similar
- User pastes content and says "what should I call this?"
- User wants to repurpose an existing article with a fresh title

## Title Rules

1. **Use numbers aggressively** — push to include a number in every title where it's plausible. Timeframes ("after 5 years"), counts ("7 patterns"), quantities ("1 skill"), rankings ("the #1 mistake") all count. Aim for numbers in at least 7 of the 10 titles.
2. **Lead with action verbs when not using a number** — "Stop Optimizing for Followers," "Track What Actually Earns," "Quit Treating Every Post the Same." The verb should be the first word or close to it.
3. **Under 20 words** (title only)
4. **Clickworthy, not sensational** — no fake urgency, no "you won't believe," no ALL CAPS shock bait
5. **Specific over generic** — "after 90 days of AI tool-building" beats "how AI changed my career"
6. **Subtitle expands, not repeats** — add new information (audience, stakes, scope, twist), never echo the title
7. **Never open with "I"** — start with a number, action verb, timeframe, or "Why / How / What"
8. **No pairs of 3 phrases** — avoid triple-clause constructions like "X, Y, and Z" or "do this, not that, and also this." They read as AI-generated filler. One clean idea per title.
9. **Preferred structure:** concrete subject → result or shift
   - ✅ *6 Months of Building AI Tools Instead of Job Searching — Results and Mindset Shifts*
   - ✅ *Stop Treating Every Post the Same — Here's What My Earnings Data Actually Shows*
   - ❌ *I Took a Sabbatical. I Did Not Rest. I Built.* (parallel fragments, AI-sounding)
   - ❌ *I Designed My Own Sabbatical Curriculum. Here's the Syllabus.* (clever pivot, artificial)
   - ❌ *Read More, Write Better, and Earn Consistently* (triple phrase, generic)

---

## Output Format

Always return exactly 10 combinations, numbered 1–10.

For each:
```
**[NUMBER]. [TITLE]**
*[Subtitle that expands and adds new info]*
```

After the 10 options, add a short note (2–3 sentences max) explaining the range — e.g., which lean more personal vs. analytical, which use numbers, which might suit a specific platform best.

---

## Process

1. **Read the input** (draft, outline, notes, topic idea) and extract:
   - Core argument or insight
   - Specific data points, timeframes, or numbers present
   - Emotional arc (struggle → discovery? before/after? myth-busting?)
   - Target reader (data professional? career pivoter? general?)

2. **Apply voice profile** based on context (ask if unclear)

3. **Generate 10 titles** with variety across:
   - Structural forms: list, confession, how-to, question, before/after, personal story lede
   - Tone range: practical/tactical → reflective/personal (spread across the 10)
   - Number usage: aim for numbers in at least 5–6 of the 10

4. **Write each subtitle** to stand alone — a reader should be able to understand the article's value from subtitle alone

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Opening with "I"** — always lead with a number, action verb, or timeframe
- **Pairs of 3 phrases** — *"Read more, write better, earn consistently"* or *"I rested. I built. I shipped."* — triple constructions feel AI-generated. One idea per title.
- **Parallel fragment pairs** — *"I Did Not Rest. I Built."* — reads as crafted, not written
- **"Here's the X" endings** — *"Here's the Syllabus," "Here's What I Found"* — overused, lazy pivot
- **Vague superlatives** — "The Ultimate Guide," "Everything You Need to Know"
- **Manufactured urgency** — "Before it's too late," "You NEED to read this"
- **Passive or flat openings** — "A Look at...", "Some Thoughts on..."
- **Title + subtitle that repeat the same idea** in different words
- **Skipping numbers** — if a number can plausibly fit, use it. Don't settle for abstract when specific is available.
